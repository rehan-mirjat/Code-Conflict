Phishing Threats

Fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information by disguising as a trustworthy entity.

View Types Protection Guide

What is Phishing?

A cyberattack that uses disguised communication (emails, texts, calls) to trick victims into revealing sensitive information.

Attack Flow:
  1. Attacker creates fake communication
  2. Victim is lured to click link/open attachment
  3. Victim enters credentials on fake site
  4. Attacker captures and abuses credentials

Phishing Attack Types

Email Phishing
Most common form

Fraudulent emails pretending to be from reputable companies to steal credentials or distribute malware.

Microsoft Account Team account@micr0soft.com
Subject: Urgent: Unusual sign-in activity

We noticed a new sign-in to your account from Ukraine. If this wasn't you, secure your account now.
SMS Phishing (Smishing)
Text message scams

Fraudulent text messages pretending to be from banks, delivery services, or government agencies.

FedEx +1 (555) 123-4567

FedEx: Your package is held at depot due to unpaid $1.99 fee. Pay now: fedex-payment.com/123

FedEx would never request payment via text
Social Media Phishing
Fake profiles and messages

Fraudulent social media accounts or messages pretending to be customer support or friends.

Netflix Support @NetflixHelpDesk

Hi! We're verifying accounts due to suspicious activity. Please confirm your account at: netflix-verify.com/login

Official accounts have verification badges
Voice Phishing (Vishing)
Phone call scams

Fraudulent phone calls pretending to be from banks, tech support, or government agencies.

Amazon Security Caller ID: "Amazon"

"This is Amazon Security. We've detected a $999 purchase on your account. Press 1 to speak with an agent and cancel this transaction."

Amazon will never call about unexpected charges

Microsoft Teams Phishing Alert New credential-stealing campaign

How the Attack Works

Attack Flow:
  1. Attacker sends fake "Teams notification" email
  2. Link leads to realistic Microsoft login page
  3. Victim enters credentials which are stolen
  4. Attacker accesses Office 365 account
  5. Used for further phishing or data theft
Red Flags:
  • Sender email doesn't match Microsoft domains
  • URL shows "teams.microsoft.com.attacker.com"
  • Generic greeting instead of your name
  • Urgent language ("Immediate action required")
  • Requests re-entering credentials
Protection:

Always check the sender's email address and hover over links before clicking. Enable MFA on all accounts.

Phishing Protection Guide

Email Protection

  1. Check sender's email address carefully
  2. Hover over links before clicking
  3. Never open unexpected attachments
  4. Look for poor grammar/spelling
  5. Enable email filtering/spam protection

General Best Practices

  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA)
  • Use a password manager
  • Keep software updated
  • Report suspicious messages
  • Regular security awareness training
